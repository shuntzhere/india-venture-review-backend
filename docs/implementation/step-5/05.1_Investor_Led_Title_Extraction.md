# Step 5.1: Improved Company Name Extraction for Investor-Led Titles

Targeted refinement of the Evidence Extraction Engine to handle article titles where the funded company appears after the investor's action phrase rather than before a funding verb.

---

## Objective

Fix company name extraction failures for investor-led article titles that were causing persistence validation failures in the Database Connector. Only `scraper/parser.py` was modified.

---

## Implementation Prompt

> This is Step 5.1 — a targeted refinement of the Evidence Extraction Engine pipeline.
> Do not modify any files outside of scraper/parser.py. Do not touch the database connector, classifier, logger, or any other module.
>
> The Evidence Extraction Engine currently fails to extract company names from investor-led article titles, causing persistence validation failures in the Database Connector.
>
> Failing examples from today's ingestion run:
> - "Boundless Ventures leads pre-seed round in Lytmus AI" → company_name returned None
> - "Yash Raj Films makes strategic investment in Rusk Media" → company_name returned None
>
> Fix required: Improve the company name extractor inside scraper/parser.py to recognise investor-led title patterns.
>
> Patterns to handle:
> - "X leads [round] in Y" → Y is the company
> - "X leads [round] for Y" → Y is the company
> - "X makes strategic investment in Y" → Y is the company
> - "X backs Y" → Y is the company
> - "X invests in Y" → Y is the company
> - "X puts [amount] in Y" → Y is the company
>
> Rules:
> - The fix must remain deterministic
> - No inference or AI reasoning
> - No assumptions beyond explicit pattern matching
> - If no pattern matches, continue returning None
> - Preserve all existing extraction behaviour for non-investor-led titles

---

## Technical Decisions

**Two-strategy architecture preserves existing behaviour**
The existing company-led extraction (splitting before a funding verb) was left completely unchanged as Strategy 1. Strategy 2 is only attempted when Strategy 1 returns None. This ensures all previously working titles continue to work identically.

**Patterns defined at module level as `_INVESTOR_LED_PATTERNS`**
Following the existing convention where all pattern constants are defined at module level (`_ROUND_PATTERNS`, `_AMOUNT_PATTERNS`, `_FUNDING_VERBS`), the new patterns are defined as `_INVESTOR_LED_PATTERNS`. This keeps them visible, auditable, and easy to extend without touching the extraction function.

**No `re.IGNORECASE` for investor-led capture groups**
Strategy 1 uses `re.IGNORECASE` because funding verbs appear in mixed case across titles. Strategy 2 does not use `re.IGNORECASE` because the company name capture group `[A-Z][\w\s\.]+?` relies on uppercase anchoring to distinguish company names (capitalised) from prepositions and lowercase words that follow the investor phrase. Applying `re.IGNORECASE` would allow lowercase words to be captured as company names.

**Non-greedy matching between investor action and company name**
The `.+?` between the action verb and the preposition (e.g., `\bleads\s+.+?\s+(?:in|for)\s+`) is non-greedy. This ensures the pattern stops at the first occurrence of "in" or "for" after the action verb — preventing it from consuming the company name itself if "in" appears multiple times in a title.

**Lookahead `(?:\s*$|[,\.])` terminates the company name capture cleanly**
After the company name, a lookahead checks for end-of-string or sentence-ending punctuation. This bounds the capture to the final entity in the title and prevents the company name from consuming trailing content in longer titles.
